# Discovery call — working sheet

For the 25 audit recipients. Twenty minutes, no slides. The report is the agenda.

---

## Before the call (2 minutes)

Open their report: `audit.aestheticbiz.site/<slug>/`

Read only three things — their **score**, their **top finding**, and their **strengths list**. The strengths matter more than the faults: you are the only person who has ever told them what their website does *well*, and it is why they took the call.

---

## The structure

**1. Open on their strength, not their fault. (2 min)**

> "Before anything else — the thing most practices your size get wrong and you got right is [strength]. I wanted to say that first, because most of what follows is small by comparison."

Why: they came expecting a sales pitch dressed as criticism. Not doing that is the whole differentiator.

**2. Ask, don't present. (5 min)**

- "How many new patients a month are you seeing at the moment?"
- "Of the people who enquire, roughly what share actually book?"
- "Who looks after the website now?"

That third question decides everything. If it is a nephew or a dormant agency, you are replacing nothing. If it is an in-house marketer, you are working alongside someone who may feel threatened — get them on the call.

**3. Their number, not yours. (5 min)**

Open the calculator with *their* figures: `aestheticbiz.site/financial`

Do not argue that the site is broken. Show what two extra patients a week does to their profit. Fixed costs do not move — rent, staff, equipment are already paid — so a small rise in patients is a large rise in profit. Let them say the number out loud.

**4. The offer. (5 min)**

> "It's ten thousand dollars. Five on acceptance, five at the pre-launch milestone. Ninety days."

Then stop talking. Do not justify it. The silence is theirs to fill.

**5. Close on a next step, not a yes. (3 min)**

> "I'll send the payment panel and the scope in writing today. Have a look, and if it's a fit, the deposit link is in there."

**If they push on price**, do not discount. Reduce scope instead:

> "I can bring the number down, but I'd be taking something out. Which of these matters least to you?"

A discount says the first number was invented. Removing scope says it was real.

### Getting paid

**Default: Wise Business. PayPal only if they insist.**

Wise issues US bank account details. From the client's side it is an ordinary
domestic US transfer — the most normal payment they can make, and one their
bookkeeper prefers. No international wire, nothing unusual to explain.

| Route on $10,000 | Cost |
|---|---|
| **Wise Business** | **≈ $50** |
| Stripe (2.9% + $0.30) | ≈ $290 |
| PayPal | ≈ $730 – $823 |

PayPal's published ZA merchant rates, for reference: 4.40% + $0.30 on
international commercial receipts ($440.60 across two instalments), then a
further 3–4% on currency conversion ($287–$382). The conversion never appears
as a line item — it is taken inside the exchange rate, which is why it is easy
to have used PayPal for years without noticing it.

**On the call, say it plainly and without apology:**

> "Bank transfer is easiest — I'll send US account details, so it's a normal
> domestic transfer at your end. If you'd rather use PayPal, that works too."

**Do not put bank details in an email body.** They go in the Payment Panel,
behind the private access link. Emailed invoices are a known wire-fraud target
in US B2B — if their finance team telephones to verify, that is a good sign,
not an obstacle.

### Stripe — not yet, and not for this

Stripe does not serve South Africa directly, and Wise's account details do not
substitute. Stripe requires a US legal entity, an EIN, a US address and a
responsible party with an SSN or ITIN. Registering without those means
declaring a business location that does not exist — Stripe detects it through
IP, device and payout patterns, and the usual outcome is a frozen account with
funds held for 90–120 days. Not a risk worth taking on the first engagement.

The legitimate route is a real US LLC (Stripe Atlas, Firstbase, doola), an EIN
and a Mercury or Relay account — roughly $300–500 to set up, plus annual fees,
and it brings US filing obligations including Form 5472, where the penalty for
missing it is $25,000. A decision for the accountant, not for one deal.

**Worth doing when** the offer becomes recurring and lower-priced — a monthly
retainer at $200–500 with card on file. Card processing earns its fee there.
It does not earn it on a $5,000 invoice a client would rather wire anyway.

## "Where are you based?"

It will come up. Answer it flatly, then move on:

> "South Africa — Durban. I've been building commercial platforms since 2001 and work with clients across the US. It's why I start my day before yours does; you tend to wake up to work that's already been done."

Do not hedge, do not volunteer it early, do not apologise. Evasion is what loses the deal — the fact almost never does, because the audit already proved you can do the work. That is the entire reason you lead with work rather than a pitch.

Timezone as an asset: SAST is 6 hours ahead of US Eastern, 9 ahead of Pacific. Your afternoon is their morning.

## "Can you just build us a website?"

> "We don't do websites — plenty of people do that well and cheaply. What we build is the part that turns a visitor into a booked, paying patient and brings them back. The site is one piece of it."

## Objections these 25 reports will produce

**"We're happy with our current site."**
> "Good — most of what I found is small. The question isn't whether the site is nice, it's how many people who land on it end up booking. Do you know that number?"

Almost nobody does. That is the opening.

**"I'll pass this to my web person."**
> "Please do, it's yours either way. Most of the day-one items they can do themselves. What I'd want to talk about is the part they can't — the loop that brings a patient back after treatment."

**"Ten thousand is a lot."**
> "It's about fourteen patients. What's your average treatment value?"

**"Send me some information."**
> "You've got the report already — that's more than I'd normally send. What would you want to see that isn't in it?"

Flushes out the real objection.

**"How do I know you're legitimate?"**
Demo practice, live and clickable: `aestheticbiz.site` — invite them to book a test appointment on it during the call. Then Star Aesthetic and CRM Solutions.

---

## Do not

- Do not open with the score. It is a judgment, and you have not earned the right yet.
- Do not read the findings aloud. They have the report; reading it is the least valuable thing you can do with twenty minutes.
- Do not discount on the call.
- Do not promise rankings, traffic figures or a specific number of patients. The reports were careful not to; do not undo that live.
- Do not disparage whoever built the site. It is often the owner, or their daughter.
