I went through giovannimedspa.com this morning and wrote up what I found.

The thing worth thirty seconds: nothing on your first screen says where you
are. No town, no address, no phone number. A patient arriving from a search
can't tell whether you're ten minutes away or two hours, and proximity is the
first filter they apply — before price, before treatment, before practitioner.

Related: no treatment is named either. Someone searching for filler or laser
can't tell from that screen whether you offer it, so they either press the
button on faith or leave.

What's genuinely good is the build. "Schedule a Consultation" is a proper
booking button rather than a browse link, the page is unusually calm and
readable on a phone, and your photography is the actual reception with your own
signage rather than a stock interior. That last one does more for trust than
most owners realise.

Two lines — your town and your main treatments — would put the information
back on a page that's already well made.
