Executive summary

The substance here is strong. The opening paragraph names the town twice, names the treatments — Botox, laser, skin rejuvenation, body contouring — and names the physician: “under the medical direction of Dr. Benjamin Lam”. Three of the four things a first-time patient needs are answered before they scroll, and “Book Now” is a solid, unmistakable button. Most practices we review answer none of them.

The presentation is working against it, and one item is a fault rather than a preference. On desktop the gift-card banner is layered over the header instead of above it: the logo is clipped through the middle and the navigation row carrying Shop Now, My Cart and Call Now is cut horizontally. On a phone the same banner pushes the top of the headline off the screen, so the first thing a visitor sees is a word beginning halfway through.

Beyond that, the headline runs to eleven words set in a decorative all-capitals face at very large size over a close-up photograph — which makes the practice's own name harder to read than the promotion sitting on top of it.

Evidence limitation: this review covers the homepage on desktop and mobile plus publicly visible information. Treatment-page depth and technical performance were not assessed, so the score is normalised across the six categories that were. Nothing here uses private analytics, and no traffic or revenue figures have been estimated.

What the practice is already doing well

The hardest questions a patient asks are already answered here. That is rarer than it should be.

  • The physician is named, with medical direction stated. (Observed) “under the medical direction of Dr. Benjamin Lam” answers both who is responsible and that a doctor is governing the clinical work. For a med spa that second point matters more than most practices realise, and few state it plainly.
  • The treatments are named specifically. (Observed) Botox, laser treatments, skin rejuvenation, body contouring, medical wellness. A patient searching for any of those can see immediately that they are in the right place.
  • The town appears twice on the first screen. (Observed) Lower Gwynedd, PA in the headline and again in the opening sentence. Proximity is the first filter an aesthetic patient applies, and it is answered without them having to look.
  • Gift cards are a working revenue channel. (Observed) A digital gift-card route is prepaid revenue from buyers who may never have visited, and it brings in new patients at no acquisition cost. Many practices never build the path at all.
  • There is a real shop, and a Call Now route beside it. (Observed) “Shop Now”, “My Cart” and “Call Now” sit together in the navigation. Retail product income between appointments and a one-tap call for the patient who will not fill in a form are both revenue most practices leave on the table. Both currently sit behind the banner fault above.

The evidence

Priority opportunities

1 · Display fault

On desktop the banner is layered over the navigation.

Evidence: At 1366×768 the gift-card bar sits on top of the header rather than above it. The Strella logo is clipped through the middle, and the navigation row — “Shop Now | My Cart | Call Now” — is cut horizontally, with only the lower half of the lettering visible.

Why it matters to a full-fee patient: This is a display fault rather than a design choice, and it lands on the two things that earn: the practice's name and the row containing Call Now and My Cart. A visitor cannot read a navigation item that is sliced in half, and a clipped logo is the first thing they see.

Recommended: The banner needs to push the header down rather than sit over it — a layering correction in the theme, not a redesign. Worth checking first because it affects every page, not only the homepage.

2 · First screen

The promotion pushes the headline off the top of the page.

Evidence: At 390×844 the gift-card banner occupies the top of the screen, and the hero headline begins mid-phrase with “AESTHETICS –”. The practice's logo and the opening words of the headline are not visible without scrolling up.

Why it matters to a full-fee patient: A visitor's first impression is a promotion followed by a sentence that starts halfway through. The practice's own name is the casualty, and a truncated first word reads as something broken rather than something offered.

Recommended: Make the banner dismissible and shorter, or move the gift-card offer below the hero. The promotion is worth having — it simply should not arrive before the practice introduces itself.

3 · Readability

Eleven words in a decorative all-capitals face.

Evidence: The headline reads “…AESTHETICS – AWARD WINNING MEDICAL SPA IN LOWER GWYNEDD, PA”, set entirely in capitals in a decorative display face at very large size, over a close-up photograph.

Why it matters to a full-fee patient: Decorative capitals at that length read slowly — the eye loses the word shapes it relies on — and the photograph behind reduces contrast further. The information is good; it is simply harder work to take in than it needs to be.

Recommended: Shorten the headline to the essentials and set the remainder in sentence case at a smaller size. “Award-winning medical spa in Lower Gwynedd” says the same thing in half the space.

4 · Decision clarity

Three promotions compete on the opening screens.

Evidence: “Send a digital gift card instantly” with a Gift Cards button at the top, “Build Your Personalized Treatment Plan” below the hero, and “August Specials Click Here!” immediately after.

Why it matters to a full-fee patient: Each is reasonable alone. Together they ask a first-time visitor to choose between a gift, a plan, a discount and a booking before they know anything about the practice — and the booking is the one that earns.

Recommended: Let Book Now own the first screen. Move gift cards and specials below the point where the practice has been introduced, so they catch the visitor who has already decided to stay.

5 · Proof

No rating or review appears before the decision.

Evidence: No patient review, star rating or review count appears on the first screen, and the “award winning” claim does not name the award.

Why it matters to a full-fee patient: The page has a named physician, which is the strongest trust signal available — and no independent voice beside it. A rating with a count is the natural companion, because it answers “how many other people agree” rather than “who is in charge”.

Recommended: Add the Google rating and review count beside Book Now, and name the award and year so the claim becomes checkable.

Limitations & methodology

This review is based on the public homepage of strellapa.com viewed on 13 August 2026 at 1366×768 and 390×844, page source retrieved the same day, and publicly visible business information.
