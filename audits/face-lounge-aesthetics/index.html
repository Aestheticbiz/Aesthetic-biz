Executive summary

There is a well-run practice behind this website, and the evidence for that is easy to find. The treatment menu is unusually well organised — RF and laser, skin health, injectables, peels, hair restoration, each with its own named treatments. There is a membership programme, a working product shop carrying named brands, a $25 offer for new clients, and a page description that names Scottsdale, the telephone number and a free consultation. The words further down the page are good too: “locally owned med spa in Scottsdale, AZ, located in the Edwards Professional Park II” is exactly the kind of specificity that earns trust.

None of it appears on the first screen. What a visitor sees instead is a close-up video of a treatment machine's control panel — a temperature readout of 45.0 °C, a timer, and the words “ELAPSED” and “LIVE TEMP”. There is no logo, no practice name, no headline and no location. A dark panel sits across the middle of the hero with nothing visible in it. On a phone the situation is worse: the navigation renders as a single horizontal row that runs past the right edge of the screen, so of eight items only Home, Treatments and Shop can be seen — Book, Memberships, About, Blog and Contact all fall outside it, and no menu button is offered in their place.

Evidence limitation: this review covers the homepage on desktop and mobile plus publicly visible page source. Individual treatment-page depth and technical performance were not assessed, so the score is normalised across the six categories that were. Nothing here uses private analytics, and no traffic or revenue figures have been estimated.

What the practice is already doing well

These are real assets, and the recommendations below are almost entirely about moving them to where they can be seen.

  • The treatment menu is properly structured. (Observed) RF and laser, skin health, injectables, peels, hair restoration — each grouped with its own named treatments, from Morpheus8 and NuEra Tight through to PRX-T33, PDO threads and BioRePeel. That is how a patient actually searches, and it is a great deal of work already done.
  • The page description is genuinely well written. (Observed) “Your top med spa and aesthetics in Scottsdale … Call (480) 597-4603 to book your free consultation!” This is the line that appears beneath the practice in search results, and it names the town, the number and the offer. Most practices leave it as boilerplate.
  • The introduction is specific in the way that earns trust. (Observed) “A locally owned med spa in Scottsdale, AZ, located in the Edwards Professional Park II … a quiet and intimate setting.” Naming the actual building is the sort of detail an invented business never supplies, and it answers the proximity question outright.
  • Memberships, a product shop and a new-client offer. (Observed) Recurring revenue, retail income between appointments, and “$25 off your first service” to convert the undecided. Three commercial mechanisms most practices in this review do not have at all.
  • The telephone number is in the desktop header. (Observed) A local 480 number, visible without scrolling, for the patient who would rather speak to someone than fill anything in.

The evidence

Priority opportunities

1 · Mobile navigation

Five of the eight menu items are off the screen, including Book.

Evidence: At 390×844 the navigation renders as one horizontal row. Only Home, Treatments and Shop are visible; Book, Memberships, About, Blog and Contact continue past the right edge. No hamburger or menu button is presented in their place.

Why it matters to a full-fee patient: Most aesthetic traffic is on a phone, and the item that has fallen off the edge is the one that earns. A visitor who wants to book has no visible way to do it, no visible telephone number on that screen, and no menu button to look for it in — so the practice's own booking page is effectively unreachable from the homepage.

Recommended: Switch the header to the mobile menu pattern the site builder already provides, so the eight items collapse behind a menu button. This is a setting rather than a rebuild, and it is the single highest-value change on this list.

2 · First screen

The opening image is a close-up of a machine's control panel.

Evidence: The desktop hero plays a video showing a device readout: “TEMP °C 45.0”, “TIME (min)”, “ELAPSED” and “LIVE TEMP 46.1”. On mobile the hero shows an out-of-focus frame of a treatment in progress. Neither carries a logo, practice name, headline or location.

Why it matters to a full-fee patient: A first-time visitor cannot tell whose website they are on. Equipment readouts are meaningful to the operator and to nobody else — a patient does not choose a practice on temperature settings, and a blurred clinical close-up asks them to identify with the procedure rather than the result.

Recommended: Put the practice name, “Scottsdale, AZ” and one line naming the main treatments over that hero, with the Book button beneath. The wording already exists further down the page — “Hello Gorgeous. Welcome to Face Lounge Aesthetics” — it simply needs to be first.

3 · Display fault

A dark panel sits across the hero with nothing in it.

Evidence: At 1366×768 a dark translucent rectangle with a small downward notch spans the centre of the hero video. No text or image is visible within it.

Why it matters to a full-fee patient: An empty container reads as something that failed to load, and it is sitting in the most valuable space on the site. If it is meant to hold a headline, that headline is not reaching anybody; if it is left over from an earlier layout, it is obscuring the video it sits on.

Recommended: Worth checking in the editor — either fill it with the practice name and headline, or remove it.

4 · Proof

The strongest claims are below the decision point.

Evidence: “Welcome to Scottsdale's 5-Star Rated Med Spa”, the locally-owned introduction and the “$25 off your first service” offer for new clients all appear below the opening screen. No practitioner is named on it.

Why it matters to a full-fee patient: A rating, a local story and a first-visit discount are the three things most likely to convert a hesitant first-time patient, and all three are currently behind a scroll that a large share of visitors will not make. The offer in particular is doing no work where it sits.

Recommended: Move the 5-star claim with its review count and the $25 new-client offer onto the first screen beside the booking button, and name the practitioner with a photograph.

Limitations & methodology

This review is based on the public homepage of faceloungeaz.com viewed on 13 August 2026 at 1366×768 and 390×844, page source retrieved the same day, and publicly visible business information. This review assessed the homepage at 1366×768 and at 390 pixels wide, together with publicly visible page source. Individual treatment-page depth and technical performance were not assessed and are excluded from the score rather than estimated; the total is normalised across the six categories reviewed (90 points available). The hero is a video, so the frame described here is the one shown during this review. Navigation items that fall outside the screen were judged on what is visible without horizontal scrolling.

No private analytics, Google Business Profile insights, booking data or revenue figures were accessed, and none have been estimated. Findings labelled Observed are directly visible; findings labelled Interpretive are professional assessment and are identified as such. No legal, HIPAA, medical board or clinical compliance judgement is offered or implied — those require professional review. Figures in the growth scenario are illustrative placeholders supplied by this report, not measurements of your practice.
