Executive summary

This is the strongest site in our current batch. “Call or Text 480-336-4195” sits in the header — giving the patient who wants to speak and the one who would rather not the same easy route. A “★★★★★ Over 450+ Five-Star Reviews” badge sits directly on the hero image, and the photograph is a real practitioner in gloves rather than a stock model. Those three things together put DHH ahead of almost every practice we review.

The gaps are small and specific. The person in that photograph is never named, so the site shows a practitioner without introducing one. “Award-Winning” appears in the headline without saying which award. And a chat widget sits over the opening paragraph on mobile, covering the sentence that lists what the practice actually does.

Evidence limitation: this review covers the homepage on desktop and mobile plus publicly visible information. Treatment-page depth and technical performance were not assessed, so the score is normalised across the six categories that were. Nothing here uses private analytics, and no traffic or revenue figures have been estimated.

What the practice is already doing well

Several of these are things we recommend to other practices. They are already in place here.

  • “Call or Text” in the header. (Observed) Offering both, in the header, on the first screen. Texting suits the patient who wants to ask about suitability or price without committing to a conversation — and a large share of aesthetic enquiries begin exactly there.
  • 450+ five-star reviews, on the hero image. (Observed) A star badge reading “Over 450+ Five-Star Reviews” placed directly over the hero. Both the volume and the placement are excellent — proof of that scale, positioned where the decision happens, is the single most persuasive thing an aesthetic site can show.
  • The hero photograph is real. (Observed) A practitioner in gloves holding a syringe, in what appears to be the practice's own room. Not a stock model. Patients recognise the difference more than most practices assume.
  • The location is in the headline. (Observed) “Med Spa in Scottsdale, AZ” — exactly what a patient searching locally needs, in the largest text on the page.

The evidence

Priority opportunities

1 · Trust

The practitioner in the photograph is never named.

Evidence: The hero shows a practitioner in gloves holding a syringe. No name, qualification or role appears with the image on the first screen.

Why it matters to a full-fee patient: The site is one line away from the strongest trust signal available. A patient looking at that photograph is already asking who she is — and being shown a clinician without being introduced to one leaves the question open at exactly the wrong moment.

Recommended: Add her name and qualification beneath or beside the photograph. Given the 450+ reviews already displayed, naming the person responsible for them compounds both signals rather than adding a separate one.

2 · Mobile

The chat widget covers the sentence listing the treatments.

Evidence: At 390×844 a “Click to send us a message” bubble is fixed over the paragraph beneath the headline, obscuring part of the line reading “Injectables, dermal fillers, … loss, and so much more”.

Why it matters to a full-fee patient: That paragraph is where a visitor learns what the practice actually offers, and it is the one thing on the screen the widget is covering. The tool intended to start conversations is hiding the reason to have one.

Recommended: Collapse the widget to a small closed bubble in the corner, and let it expand only on tap or after the visitor has scrolled.

3 · Message

“Award-Winning” does not say which award.

Evidence: The headline reads “Award-Winning Med Spa in Scottsdale, AZ”. No award, body or year is named on the first screen.

Why it matters to a full-fee patient: An unnamed award is read as a figure of speech rather than a fact, so it works no harder than any other adjective — while a named one is checkable and therefore persuasive. The practice appears to have the substance; it is the specificity that is missing.

Recommended: Name it: the award, who gave it and when. If there is more than one, the most recognisable belongs in the headline and the rest on the About page.

4 · Conversion

There is no booking button on the first screen.

Evidence: The first screen offers “Call or Text” in the header. No online booking or enquiry button appears above the fold on either desktop or mobile.

Why it matters to a full-fee patient: Call and text are strong and cover most people. The remaining group is the patient who will not phone a stranger and does not want to start a conversation yet — they want to pick a time and be done. With 450+ reviews doing the persuading, that patient is arriving ready and finding no way to finish.

Recommended: Add a booking button beside the call-or-text option. It serves a different patient rather than replacing the one already served well.

No legal, HIPAA, medical board or clinical compliance judgement is offered or implied — those require professional review. Figures in the growth scenario are illustrative placeholders supplied by this report, not measurements of your practice.
