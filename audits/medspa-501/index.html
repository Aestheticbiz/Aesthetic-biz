Executive summary

The positioning here is unusually clear. “Chapel Hill Med Spa for Aesthetics & Longevity Medicine” names the town and stakes out a specific territory in one line, and the sentence beneath it — “medical-led aesthetic and wellness care in Chapel Hill designed to help you look, feel, and function at your best” — repeats the town and adds the medical framing. On desktop a solid black “Book Your Appointment” sits in the top right where it cannot be missed.

Two things work against it once the visitor is on a phone. That strong booking button moves into the menu, leaving only “Aesthetic Solutions” and “Wellness & Functional Medicine Solutions” visible — and both of those lead to more reading rather than an appointment. Below them the cookie panel takes the bottom third of the screen and covers the second one. The headline also runs to five lines at phone width, which pushes everything else down before the visitor has been given a reason to stay.

Evidence limitation: this review covers the homepage on desktop and mobile plus publicly visible information. Treatment-page depth and technical performance were not assessed, so the score is normalised across the six categories that were. Nothing here uses private analytics, and no traffic or revenue figures have been estimated.

What the practice is already doing well

The positioning is the hardest thing on this list to get right, and it is already right.

  • The headline stakes out a specific territory. (Observed) “Chapel Hill Med Spa for Aesthetics & Longevity Medicine.” Longevity medicine is a real differentiator and naming it in the headline attracts a patient with a different, generally longer-term relationship than one booking a single treatment.
  • The town is named twice before the scroll. (Observed) Once in the headline and again in the supporting line. Proximity is the first filter an aesthetic patient applies, and it is answered here without them having to look for it.
  • “Medical-led” does real work. (Observed) It separates the practice from the beauty-salon end of the market in two words, and it sets up the longevity medicine claim rather than leaving it floating.
  • The desktop booking button is exactly where it should be. (Observed) Solid black, top right, permanently visible. Unmistakable, and it asks for the appointment rather than inviting a browse.
  • “At every stage of life” is well judged. (Observed) A quiet line that widens the audience without excluding anybody, and it sits naturally with the longevity positioning rather than being bolted on.

The evidence

Priority opportunities

1 · Mobile

On a phone, neither visible button asks for the appointment.

Evidence: At 390×844 the header carries the logo and a menu button; the desktop “Book Your Appointment” is not shown. The two buttons on the screen are “Aesthetic Solutions” and “Wellness & Functional Medicine Solutions”, and the second is partly covered by the consent panel.

Why it matters to a full-fee patient: The one action that earns has been moved behind a menu button, and the two that remain lead to more reading. A patient who arrived ready to book must first find the menu, open it and look for the option — and every additional step loses a share of the people who were willing.

Recommended: Keep a compact Book Your Appointment in the mobile header beside the menu button. It is the single highest-value change on this list.

2 · Proof

Nothing on the first screen can be checked by the visitor.

Evidence: No rating, review count, credential or named clinician appears on the opening screen, on either desktop or mobile. “Medical-led” does not say led by whom.

Why it matters to a full-fee patient: The positioning is built on medical authority, and the page never shows the person that authority belongs to. Longevity medicine in particular is an area where patients are cautious about who is prescribing — and the practice is asking for that trust without introducing anybody.

Recommended: Name the physician with a photograph and qualification on the first screen, and add the Google rating with its review count beside the booking button. The medical positioning is the asset; a name is what makes it real.

3 · Mobile

The consent panel takes the bottom third of the phone screen.

Evidence: At 390×844 the cookie notice begins immediately below the first button and continues past the foot of the screen, covering the lower half of the second button.

Why it matters to a full-fee patient: It arrives at the moment the visitor is deciding, and it partially obscures one of only two available actions. A partly covered button also reads as a broken layout rather than a legal notice.

Recommended: Reduce it to a slim bar across the foot of the screen, keeping the same choices.

4 · Readability

The headline runs to five lines at phone width.

Evidence: “Chapel Hill Med Spa for Aesthetics & Longevity Medicine” wraps across five lines in a large serif face, occupying roughly a third of the screen above the supporting sentence.

Why it matters to a full-fee patient: The wording is good, but at that size it becomes a wall rather than a line, and it pushes the supporting sentence and both buttons further down. A headline that takes a third of the screen leaves little room for the reason to act.

Recommended: Reduce the mobile type size a step so it settles onto three lines. Same words, considerably more room beneath them.

Limitations & methodology

This review is based on the public homepage of medspa501.com viewed on 13 August 2026 at 1366×768 and 390×844, page source retrieved the same day, and publicly visible business information. This review assessed the homepage at 1366×768 and at 390 pixels wide, together with publicly visible information. No private analytics, Google Business Profile insights, booking data or revenue figures were accessed, and none have been estimated. Findings labelled Observed are directly visible; findings labelled Interpretive are professional assessment and are identified as such. No legal, HIPAA, medical board or clinical compliance judgement is offered or implied — those require professional review. Figures in the growth scenario are illustrative placeholders supplied by this report, not measurements of your practice.
